Privacy Policy

Roamkeep · com.roamkeep.app · last updated 11 October 2026

No server of ours holds your family's data. Every family runs its own private database, which they create and control. Your location is sent to that database. None of it is sent to the developer, and the few other services your phone uses are listed below.

The developer of Roamkeep cannot see your location, your saved places, your family members, or your messages, not because of a promise but because there is no system under the developer's control that receives them.

Who is responsible for your data

When your family sets up Roamkeep, you create your own project on Supabase, a third-party hosting provider. Your family is the controller of that data. You choose the region it is stored in, you hold the credentials, and you can delete the entire project at any time, which erases everything at once.

The developer of Roamkeep has no account access, no credentials, and no technical route into your project.

When the app sets up your family's server, it asks for a temporary access token from your Supabase account. The token is kept only in the app's memory, sent only to Supabase, never saved, and forgotten when setup ends. The app then reminds you to delete it in Supabase.

Roamkeep contains no analytics, no advertising, no trackers, and no crash reporting. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Why the app needs background location

Roamkeep's main purpose is to tell your family when you arrive home, or at school, or at any place they have saved, and to show your position on the family map. Those things have to keep working when the app is closed, or the app cannot do the one job it exists to do.

Roamkeep asks for this permission explicitly, explains it in the app before asking, and shows a permanent notification in your phone's status bar whenever it is recording your location. If that notification can't be shown, Roamkeep doesn't record. With notifications turned off for Roamkeep, it stops sharing your location, your family sees "Not sharing: notifications are off", and the app shows you how to turn them back on. Adults can pause their own sharing from inside the app for a set time (an hour, two, or the rest of the evening), and it turns back on by itself when the time is up. Children can't pause, but anyone can disconnect their phone, and the family sees that they did.

What the developer runs

  • The notification relay, described below. It is the only service the developer runs for the app itself.
  • The Roamkeep websites, roamkeep.app and get.roamkeep.app. They have no analytics and set no cookies. Like most websites, their hosting provider keeps standard access logs (IP address, page, time and browser) for 90 days. An invite link's family details are in the part of the address that browsers never send, so they never appear in those logs.
  • A demonstration server for app store review. It contains only invented people and invented places.

The notification relay

The relay wakes up phones when there is something for them to fetch: a notification, or a change to your family's saved places. It exists because notification delivery on Android has to be signed with a key belonging to the published app, which a family's own database cannot hold.

The relay never receives the content of a notification. It receives a list of device tokens and sends each one a fixed, meaningless wake-up signal. Your phone then fetches the actual details from your family's own database and writes the notification text itself, on the device.

The relay therefore sees device tokens, how many there are, and when. It cannot see names, places, coordinates, messages, or even what kind of event occurred. Every wake-up is identical, and many of them produce no notification at all. It stores nothing and logs nothing.

The relay's hosting provider applies a rate limit to it and keeps a short record of requests it blocks. Those records identify the family server that made the call, never your phone. Your device never contacts the relay directly.

The relay's complete source code is published, and this claim can be checked against it.

Other services your phone talks to

  • Google. Wake-ups are delivered through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, which sees your phone's notification token and when a wake-up arrives, and nothing about your family. Your phone's position is worked out by Google Play services on the phone itself, under your phone's own location settings.
  • OpenStreetMap. The map pictures come straight from OpenStreetMap's tile servers. Like any website, they see your phone's IP address and which part of the map is being shown, and the app tells them the request comes from Roamkeep, as their rules ask. They don't see who you are or anyone's location in your family. Their privacy policy applies.

What stays on your phone

To keep working while the app is closed, Roamkeep keeps a few things on your phone itself: your sign-in to your family's server, your family's saved places, your last few positions (so it can tell real movement from GPS drift), arrivals and departures waiting to be sent when the signal comes back, and a diagnostic record of what the app did, which you can read in the app and which keeps nothing older than 7 days. Android does not back any of it up. Disconnecting the device or signing out deletes it. Notifications the app has shown stay in your notification shade until you dismiss them.

Who can see your location

Only the members of your Keep. The database enforces this with row-level security, so it is a property of the data itself rather than of the app: a modified or malicious client still cannot read another family's rows.

Roamkeep does not sell data, does not share data with advertisers, and contains no advertising or analytics code.

Children

Roamkeep is designed for families and is intended to be set up by a parent or guardian, who invites the rest of the household. Members marked as children cannot pause their own tracking, leave the Keep or delete their own account; adults can. A child can always disconnect their device, and the family sees that they did. A child can ask the Keep's owners to delete their account (Settings → Leave and delete my account), and an owner completes it from the Family tab. Roamkeep is not directed at children as an audience and children cannot create a Keep of their own without an invitation.

Because your family holds all of the data, a parent can remove a child from the Keep, which deletes their account and everything recorded about them on your family's server, or delete the entire database at any time, without asking anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Location history is deleted automatically after 7 days.
  • Check-ins (arrivals, departures and SOS alerts) are deleted automatically after 7 days. Every phone that is sharing runs this clean-up once a day, and so does your family's server where it is set up to.
  • Leaving your family (Settings → Leave and delete my account) deletes your account on your family's server: your sign-in, your membership, your location history and your check-ins. Places you added stay with the family. The same happens when an owner removes someone, or declines someone's request to join. Two people can't leave this way: a member marked as a child, who asks the owners instead, and the only remaining owner of a Keep with other people in it, who must make someone else an owner first.
  • Deleting the Supabase project destroys everything your family ever recorded on it, immediately and permanently, subject to Supabase's own retention of backups.
  • Disconnecting a device (Settings → Disconnect this device) clears the app's stored credentials and everything else it keeps on that phone, and stops all tracking there. Your family is told: that device is marked offline and your status is set to ‘Disconnected this device’, so switching tracking off is visible to them rather than looking like a phone that simply went quiet. If the phone has no connection at that moment, the app says your family couldn't be told.

Because the developer holds no copy of your data, there is no deletion request to send to us. Deleting it is in your family's hands.

Security

All traffic between the app and your database is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Access is controlled by row-level security enforced by the database. Setup links carry your family's server address in the part of a URL that browsers never transmit to a web server, so it does not appear in anyone's access logs.
